## 1. False positives and false negatives — intent detection

Method: 52 `(category, answer, expected quality)` cases labelled by reading each answer as a recruiter would, **frozen
before the engine was changed**; the unmodified engine's predictions were saved separately. Groups separate the
documented gaps from the negatives a fix must not break.

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | 25/52 = 48.1% (95% CI 35–61%) | **49/52 = 94.2%** (95% CI 84–98%) |
| Recall of "ok" (valid answers accepted) | 18.2% | **93.9%** |
| Precision of "ok" | 100% | 96.9% |

The error type was almost entirely **false negatives** (valid answers called vague) — a fairness-relevant error, since it
silently penalises candidates whose answers are short and correct. All 27 initial errors were of this kind (25 since fixed, 2 deliberately left).

| Group | n | Correct before → after |
|---|---|---|
| Degree abbreviations (B.Sc, M.Sc, B.Com, MBA, BCA, MCA, BBA, PhD, B.E., M.Tech, MBBS, LLB, B.Pharm …) | 14 | 0 → **14** |
| Bare place names (Chennai, Kochi, Thiruvananthapuram, "Pune, Maharashtra" …) | 11 | 0 → **11** |
| Controls already correct | 7 | 7 → 7 |
| Hedges, non-answers, work-mode words, wrong-category answers (the negatives) | 17 | 17 → **17** |
| Hard, deliberately not fixed ("BE Mechanical", "B.A English") | 2 | 0 → 0 |
| Documented trade-off ("Banana") | 1 | 1 → **0** |

**Net +24 hides one regression: 25 fixed, 1 newly broken.** The one is "Banana" for a location question — without a
city list a nonsense word cannot be told from an unfamiliar town. It is documented, pinned by a test, and downstream
completeness scoring (Day 26) still catches content-free answers. A city list was rejected on purpose: it would accept
the cities someone thought to include and reject every smaller town, an uneven rule; the stoplist treats all place names alike.

**Residual errors (3 of 52):** `BE Mechanical` and `B.A English` (undotted "BE" and "BA" are ordinary English words;
adding them would create false positives) and `Banana`.

### Two honest corrections made along the way

1. **An over-correction caught by existing tests.** The first draft accepted "Hybrid" and "Onsite" as locations.
   Day 30's tests (which pin its deliberate decision that work-mode words are not a location) failed. The stoplist now
   includes work-mode words.
2. **A labelling error of mine, disclosed.** I had labelled "Remote" as a valid location. The same Day 30 decision
   contradicts that. I corrected the label and recorded it in the benchmark's `revisions` field rather than editing
   silently; the frozen before-snapshot is unaffected. This changed the before-score from 24/52 to 25/52.

**Limits:** n = 52; labelled by the same author as the fix, so it is a regression-and-gap benchmark, not an independent
estimate of production accuracy. A second person should label additional cases before this number is trusted.

## 2. Scoring thresholds — evidence, not re-tuning

The harness sweeps the ATS shortlist and review cutoffs over 895 settings and re-picks them with each pair held out
(leave-one-out), to show how much of the headline accuracy is memorisation.

| Category | n | Current cutoffs | Correct (current / best attainable) | Settings tying for best | Leave-one-out vs in-sample |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tech | 7 | shortlist 47, review 25 | 5/7 / 5/7 | **78 of 895**; shortlist window **47–49** | **3/7 vs 5/7** (optimism +0.29) |
| Business | 5 | shortlist 55, review 25 | 5/5 / 5/5 | 771 of 895; window 35–61 | 5/5 vs 5/5 |

**Conclusion: no threshold changed.** The current cutoffs already achieve the best attainable accuracy, and the two tech
misses were shown on Day 20 to be unfixable by thresholds. But the tech shortlist cutoff of 47 sits at the **edge of a 3-point
window**, and roughly two of its five correct pairs are attributable to fitting rather than generalisation — **treat it as
provisional**. The business cutoff is unconstrained by the data (the five pairs separate trivially), so the data can neither
confirm nor refine it.

**Sample size:** an 80% accuracy measured on 30 pairs is 63–90% (±14%); on 100 pairs 71–87% (±8%); on 200, 74–85% (±6%).
Collecting **≥ 30 labelled pairs per category** is the single most valuable next step for ATS thresholds.

Thresholds in Days 51–53 (70/50, the 40 floors, 55/50 confidence, 75/50 strengths) have **no labelled data at all**; they are
audited for consistency (section 4) but cannot be tuned.

### 4b. Threshold registry — two contradictions found

**Finding 1 — the ATS round is on a different scale from the others (my Day 51 design flaw).** The ATS engine's scores
structurally top out around 50–60 (a clean tech match is ~50), which is why it has its own cutoffs (47 / 55) instead of 70.
Day 51 nevertheless treated every round's 0–100 as comparable. Measured on the 12 human-labelled pairs:

| | Raw ATS score | Zone-aligned ATS score |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement with the human zone, read at the aggregate's 70/50 bands | **6/12** | **10/12** (= the ATS's own accuracy) |
| Human-**shortlisted** pairs reaching the selection band (70) | **0 of 4** | **4 of 4** |
| Hiring Fit of a shortlisted pair when every other round is 80 | 76.95 – 78.15 | 79.14 – 79.43 |

The fix (`parsers/scoring_calibration.py`) is a monotone piecewise-linear alignment **anchored on the ATS's own existing
cutoffs** (review cutoff → 50, shortlist cutoff → 70, 0 → 0, 100 → 100), plus honouring the ATS's hard skill-relevance floor.
It reorders no candidate and learns no new number. **How to read the evidence:** the 6/12 is independent evidence of the
mismatch; the 10/12 equals the ATS's own accuracy by construction (the alignment reproduces its zone decisions), so it shows the
scales now *agree*, not that either is more accurate. The first draft ignored the skill floor and read three labelled REJECT
pairs as REVIEW; the 12-pair check caught it.

It is **opt-in** — `compute_from_round_outputs(…, ats_scale="zone_aligned")` — so no earlier day's results change silently.
**Recommendation:** switch the default after review. The effect is modest on a typical candidate (+1.3 to +2.2 Hiring Fit points
here) but larger for a candidate whose other rounds are strong, where the raw scale can trigger a spurious disagreement
review (a unit test demonstrates this).

**Finding 2 — Day 52's confidence floors contradict its own rationale.** Day 52 says shaky evidence must never be enough to
recommend *rejection* because a wrong rejection costs more — yet the rejection floor (50) is **below** the selection floor (55).
**Not changed** (a policy choice with no data; existing tests pin it). Recommended: `DecisionPolicy(min_reject_confidence=60)`.
Effect: **0 of the 9 original Day 52 demo candidates change**; of one extra shaky-evidence case constructed to test it, 1 changes
from REJECTED to HOLD (confidence 53.2). It matters only for rejections with confidence between 50 and 60.

Other relationships checked and holding: hold < selection; Day 52 defaults = Day 51 thresholds; strength ≥ selection;
weakness = hold; critical floors < hold; borderline margins do not overlap.

## 5. Processing speed

Profiled first, optimised only where the profile pointed.

| Stage | Cost | Action |
|---|---|---|
| ATS scoring (semantic similarity = **75%** of it) | ~2.7–5.2 ms / pair (machine-dependent) | **Optimised** |
| Day 51 aggregate + Day 52 decision + Day 53 report + JSON/Markdown/HTML exports (five-round candidate) | **~1.3 ms / candidate** (~750/s) | **Left alone** — optimising it would add risk for no benefit |

The ATS hotspot: `SemanticMatcher.embed` re-vectorised the *same* text for every pair (a typical run scores one job description
against many resumes), and every comparison paid sklearn's `cosine_similarity` input validation on vectors that are already unit
length. `parsers/semantic_matcher.py` (+36 / −4 lines): `embed` is memoised in a bounded (1,024), thread-safe LRU cache, and
`similarity` takes the dot product directly (TF-IDF vectors are L2-normalised, so cosine similarity *is* the dot product).

| | Legacy | Optimised |
|---|---|---|
| 64 pairs (16 resumes × 4 job descriptions), cold cache | ~175–330 ms | ~64–120 ms |
| Speed-up | | **2.6× – 3.1×** (machine-dependent) |
| Scores | | **bit-identical** on all 64 pairs (every component, full float precision) |

Equivalence is also tested: every ordered pair of ten varied texts (including empty, stop-word-only and gibberish) equals
sklearn's own cosine exactly, and six threads hammering a small cache return the same scores.

## 8. Known limits and recommended next steps

3. Other rounds (screening, HR, technical, machine test) may have their own scale quirks; they cannot be checked without labelled data.
4. Remaining intent gaps: undotted "BE"/"BA", and nonsense place names.
5. Still true: no hiring-outcome data, no bias audit, the machine-test round rests on a sandbox that does not exist.

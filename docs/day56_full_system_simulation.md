## 1. How the simulation works

```
resume file (DOCX/PDF) -> real extractor -> ATS score + zone -> screening call (conversation controller)
   -> HR interview score -> technical interview score (technical roles only)
   -> cross-round aggregation (raw AND zone-aligned ATS scale) -> final recommendation -> recruiter report
```
Design choices that matter for reading the results:

* **Two readings of each journey.** *Diagnostic:* every stage runs for every candidate so stages can be compared.
  *Funnel:* what the PRD would do — only a shortlisted candidate gets a call, and a candidate failing a stage stops. The
  stage cutoffs (50 for screening, HR and technical) are **assumed**; the PRD names the gates but gives no numbers.
* **Candidates answer the question actually asked.** The conversation controller is run turn by turn, so when it re-asks
  (after judging an answer off-topic) the candidate gives a short re-ask answer. Technical answers are keyed by the tier
  the candidate is actually given. My first version fed one scripted line per question and the conversation fell out of
  step — the screening scores were ~30 for everyone. That was **my scripting error**, not an engine fault, and is why the
  isolation probe in section 4 exists (to separate the engine's reading of an answer from conversation-flow effects).
* **The clock is frozen** (`2026-10-30`) inside the simulation so results do not change with the day it is run; no
  existing module was edited. (The experience parser reads "Present" from today's date — finding F5.)
* **The 8 candidates are scripted.** Their acceptable outcomes were written **before** the run. They are the author's design
  intent, not independent human judgement and not real candidate data.
* **I made one wiring mistake worth recording.** The first run computed the ATS zone without the skill-relevance floor the
  project's own eligibility engine applies, so 5 of 12 zones disagreed with the human labels and two looked calendar-sensitive.
  After copying the project's own derivation the agreement is 10/12 (= the Day 54 record) and ATS zones do not drift. The
  tests pin both facts.

## 4. Inconsistencies found (ranked)

| # | Finding | Evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **No ATS gate in the decision.** A candidate the ATS rejects (and a human had rejected) can be SELECTED on interview strength | S8: ATS 27.7 = REJECT; raw fit **69.0** (1.0 under the selection threshold); under the zone-aligned scale **74.5 → SELECTED**. Aligned scaling adds 1.8–5.5 points; the decision engine fired no rule | serious |
| F2 | **Technical scoring is keyword-driven.** It cannot tell a correct explanation in plain words from a wrong one, and rewards keyword lists | one answer varied, all else equal: rubric phrases **56.8**; keyword list with no sentences **53.0**; correct paraphrase **43.5**; confidently wrong **42.5** | serious (accuracy) |
| F3 | **Natural self-introductions are judged off-topic.** An introduction that mentions role, location or school is read as another question | 4 of 56 answers read alone (the introductions of S1, S2, S6, S7); those four candidates needed 2–4 extra turns | warning |
| F4 | **Resume "Present" depends on today's date**, so the technical question tier moves with the calendar | same resumes, 2025-01-01 vs 2026-10-30: **2 of 4** technical resumes go intermediate → advanced; ATS zones 0 of 12 change (max score shift 1.7) | warning |
| F5 | **Stated vs computed experience disagree** in 7 of 8 journeys, and the tier is taken from the dates, not the claim | e.g. S1: "3 years" stated, 5.3 from dates; tier advanced; a 3-year claim would be intermediate | warning |
| F6 | **A false experience claim is caught only by this simulation.** The decision engine has no input for it | S5 claims 8 years against 3 stated / 5.3 computed: flagged `EXP_CLAIM_VS_RESUME` here; S5's outcome was `hold_review` because its technical score was low, not because of the claim | serious |
| F7 | **ATS verdict and interviews disagree** | S4: ATS review, interview average 77 (possible false negative); S3: ATS shortlist, technical 14 (resume did not predict interviews) | warning |
| F8 | **Round spread ≥ 30 points** in 6 of 8 journeys, almost always technical vs everything else | S3: screening 82 vs technical 14 | info/warning |
| F9 | **Upload failures surface as raw library errors**, and the PRD's DOC format is unsupported | `.doc` and `.txt` → `ValueError`; corrupt PDF → `PdfminerException`; zero-byte DOCX → `BadZipFile`; a blank DOCX returns empty text with warnings and no error | warning |

Clean results: PDF and DOCX uploads scored **identically** to plain text on all 12 pairs (max difference 0.0) — *on clean
synthetic files built from the same text; real two-column, table-heavy or scanned resumes are not tested here*.

## 5. System performance analysis

Measured on the scoring layer only: 5 repeats of all 8 journeys, this machine (numbers move a little from run to run).

| Stage | Median | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| upload (DOCX text extraction) | ~7 ms | file parsing |
| HR interview scoring | ~6 ms | 6 answers |
| screening call | ~4 ms | 7–12 turns |
| ATS | ~1.5 ms | resume vs JD |
| technical, aggregation, decision, report | < 0.5 ms each | — |
| **whole journey** | **~20 ms** (slowest ~22–37 ms across runs) | ≈ 2,400–2,900 candidates/min per process |

* The scoring logic is **not a bottleneck** and will not be the system's latency. Real cost will be speech-to-text,
  text-to-speech, video and network, plus the conversation itself: **8–12 turns per screening call**, so the candidate's
  time, not the machine's, dominates. None of that is simulated here.
* The two slowest stages swap places between runs (upload vs HR scoring); do not read a ranking into 1–2 ms differences.
* One-off cost: the semantic matcher builds in ~7 ms on 20 documents; it will grow with the corpus.
* Not measured: memory, concurrency, large resumes, cold-start, any network call. This is not a load test.

## 6. Improvement recommendations (prioritised)

**Priority 1 — decisions and accuracy**
1. **Add an ATS guard to the decision engine** (F1): a candidate in the ATS REJECT zone cannot be SELECTED without a person
   (cap at `hold_review`, with the reason stated). Expected effect on the simulation: S8 becomes `hold_review` on both
   scales. Needs a product decision because Days 51–52 deliberately made the ATS one weighted round; the evidence is that
   its weight (25 % for non-technical) can be outvoted by a 60 % HR round.
2. **Make technical scoring meaning-aware** (F2): penalise answers with no sentence structure, add known-misconception
   phrases as negative evidence, accept paraphrase (e.g. via the semantic matcher already in the project), and route
   low-confidence answers to a human. Re-run `technical_phrasing_probe`; the characterisation test should then be inverted
   (a correct paraphrase must beat a keyword list).
3. **Accept mixed content in introductions** (F3): an introduction legitimately mentions role, location and school; only
   reject it if it answers a different question *instead of* introducing the candidate.

**Priority 2 — integrity and reproducibility**
4. **Give the experience parser an injectable reference date** (F4): replace `date.today()` with an `as_of` argument
   defaulting to today; the simulation's `frozen_today` is a stopgap, not a fix.
5. **Decide which experience figure drives the technical tier and flag disagreement** (F5): stated, computed from dates, or
   claimed in the call. At minimum surface the difference to the reviewer instead of silently choosing.
6. **Feed cross-stage inconsistencies into `human_review_reasons`** (F6/F7) so a claim mismatch or an ATS-vs-interview
   divergence reaches the recruiter inside the report. `find_inconsistencies` is written to be lifted into the aggregator.
7. **Wrap upload failures in one typed error with a candidate-readable message**, and either support `.doc` or reject it
   explicitly (F9). Treat "empty text" as a failure, not a successful empty resume.

**Priority 3 — measurement and scope**
8. **Collect real human ratings** with the worksheet (2–3 recruiters, the 8 scripted candidates first, then real cases).
   Until then the final-decision comparison is unmeasured.
9. **Add the machine-test stage** to the simulation so technical journeys can reach a final decision, or accept and document
   that the specified journey ends `pending_evidence` for technical roles.
10. **Have the product owner set the funnel gate cutoffs** (currently an assumed 50) and **measure real end-to-end latency**
    in a staging environment with speech and video attached.

## 7. Limits

* **Scripted candidates, not real ones.** Their quality is something I wrote; the *mechanisms* found (F1–F9) are what carry
  over, not the particular scores.
* **No independent human judgement** for HR, technical or final outcomes; only the 12 ATS labels are human, and those were
  used to calibrate the ATS.
* **Funnel gate cutoffs are assumed.** Technical journeys end `pending_evidence` because the brief omits the machine test.
* **Upload fidelity was tested on clean synthetic DOCX/PDF only.**
* **Timings are the scoring layer only**; there is no speech, video, network or load.
* **Findings F2 and F3 depend on rule-based engines** that may be improved; the characterisation test documents the current
  behaviour and will need updating when they are.
* The author's expectations were fixed before the run; the first run's two scripting mistakes (one-line-per-question
  candidates, a missing skill floor) are described in section 1 and were corrected before any result was reported.

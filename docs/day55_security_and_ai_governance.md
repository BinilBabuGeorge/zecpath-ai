## 1. AI compliance design

### 1.1 What the platform does that makes this matter
The PRD describes AI that scores people, interviews them on video, analyses behaviour, records them, clones voices
(with consent) and sells recruiters paid access to the results (Phases 10, 22–25, 48, 73–75, 90, 100). That is
personal data about people who cannot easily say no, used to make decisions about their livelihood. The design
therefore starts from five commitments, each enforced in code rather than promised in prose:

| Commitment | Where it is enforced |
|---|---|
| A rejection is never automatic | Day 52 `finalize_*`; Day 55 `AuditLog.log_finalized` refuses an unnamed-human rejection; access rule R6 withholds `CONFIRM_REJECTION` from the AI `service` role |
| Every score and decision is reconstructable | Score log carries round, scores, model version, source module, config version; decision log carries outcome, confidence, rules fired |
| Personal content stays out of the evidence log | `AuditPayloadError` on forbidden keys (any depth) and on long free text |
| Nothing is used without a basis | Consent gate on recordings/transcripts; default-deny; purposes never bundled |
| Data does not outlive its purpose | Retention policy; withdrawal beats the clock; expired data is not served |

### 1.2 Consent design
* **One consent per purpose**: `ai_screening`, `ai_interview_recording`, `voice_cloning`, `talent_pool`,
  `model_improvement`. Agreeing to screening does not imply recording or voice cloning (PRD Phase 25 requires
  separate voice-cloning consent). `model_improvement` is never assumed.
* **Explicit acts only**: `explicit_opt_in` or `signed_form`. Pre-ticked / implied methods are rejected in code.
* **Tied to the notice version** the candidate saw. Changing `required_notice_version` makes older consents stale.
* **History is kept**, never overwritten; the latest event wins; events must be recorded in time order.
* **Withdrawal is real**: it updates the registry, writes an audit entry, returns immediate DELETE actions for the
  data that depended on the purpose, and makes the next access attempt fail with `CONSENT_required`.
* **Two honest caveats built into behaviour.** (1) In hiring, consent can fail the "freely given" test, because a
  candidate may fear refusing costs the job. So a refusal for an AI purpose returns `fallback="human_process"` and
  the calling product **must** offer a non-AI route. (2) Consent is one lawful basis among several; counsel decides
  which applies per jurisdiction.

### 1.3 Automated decision-making
The system is built so that a person is always in the decision. Explanations exist at every round (Day 43:
*Explainability — Ready*); the decision log keeps the rule trace so an affected person or regulator can be shown how
an outcome arose. Fairness tooling (Day 15 ATS fairness checks; Day 43 transcript demographic scrubber) is unchanged;
Day 55 adds that **analytics may read only scrubbed transcripts** (a validated storage invariant).

### 1.4 Retention policy (defaults — configurable, to be confirmed by counsel)
| Category | Clock starts | Keep | At expiry | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Recording (video/audio) | final decision | 30 days | delete | Most sensitive; only needed for a short dispute window |
| Transcript | final decision | 90 days | delete | Needed to explain a decision; free text may hold special-category data |
| Resume | final decision | 180 days | delete | Longer only with talent-pool consent |
| Report | final decision | 180 days | delete | Derived judgements about a person; no longer than its inputs |
| Scores | final decision | 365 days | **anonymise** | Aggregate fairness statistics survive once unlinked from the person |
| Consent record | final decision | 730 days | delete | Proof of consent must outlive the data it covered |
| Audit log | collection | 1095 days | review | Evidence; contains only scores and pseudonymous refs; must outlive everything above (validated) |

Rules the engine applies: no trigger date → "awaiting trigger", never guessed; legal hold → skipped **and reported**;
withdrawn consent → immediate action regardless of the clock. Demo (record set of 8): day 45 → 1 due (recording);
day 120 → 2 due (recording, transcript); day 400 → 5 due (recording, transcript, resume, report delete; scores
anonymise). The held record and the awaiting-trigger record are reported on every date.

**Erasure vs an unchangeable log.** Erasing a candidate must not mean editing the audit chain (that would defeat
it). Instead logs carry a keyed pseudonym (`cref_…`); erasing the person = destroying the ref → identity mapping.
The log then still proves what was done, but can no longer be linked to anyone.

## 2. Security framework

### 2.1 Threats and controls
| Asset | Threat | Control | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audit log | Edit / delete / reorder a past entry | SHA-256 hash chain; `verify()` names the first bad entry | **Built, tested** |
| Audit log | Truncate the tail; rewrite the whole chain | External anchor (head hash + length) and optional HMAC key | **Logic built**; anchor holder not chosen |
| Transcripts, recordings, reports | Disclosure to the wrong party | Role matrix + tenant isolation + own-data-only + paid unlock + consent + retention checks | **Built, tested** (logic); not wired to an API |
| Transcripts, reports | Theft of storage | Envelope encryption at rest, TLS in transit, keys in a separate managed service, per-tenant scope, rotation ≤ 365 d | **Plan + validator only** |
| Candidate identity | Linking logs to a person | Keyed pseudonyms; identity in one separate store | Pseudonym function **built**; store planned |
| Deleted data | Surviving in backups | Delete + key destruction (crypto-shredding); backup expiry ≤ 35 d | **Plan + validator only** |
| Decisions | Automated rejection | R6, `log_finalized` guard, Day 52 rules | **Built, tested** |
| Admin / auditor accounts | Over-broad access to content | R4: oversight roles see metadata and logs, never content | **Built, tested** |
| Sessions, login, API gateway | Impersonation | Authentication, MFA, rate limits | **Not built** — `Actor` is assumed already authenticated |
| Incidents | Late or no response | Outline in section 3.4 | **Document only** |

### 2.2 Access control summary (default matrix)
| Role | May do | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate | Read/edit/delete own resume; read own transcript, score, decision, preview; manage own consent | Anyone else's data; recordings; full reports; scores written |
| Recruiter | Read resume/transcript/recording/score/preview of **own company**; read & export **unlocked** full report; override or confirm a decision | Other companies' data; locked full content; change policy |
| Company admin | As recruiter for reports/decisions (no transcripts/recordings), plus company policy config | Other companies; recordings |
| Platform admin | Scores, previews, decisions, audit log, policy config, consent records (metadata) | Any personal content; writing/deleting the audit log |
| Auditor | Scores, decisions, audit log, consent records, policy (read-only) | Any personal content; any write |
| AI service | Read inputs; write scores, decisions, reports, **append** to audit log | Confirm a rejection; read/modify anything outside its role |

Rules in evaluation order: R5 audit append-only → R6 rejection is human → role matrix → R4 no content for oversight
roles → R1 tenant isolation → R2 own data only → R3 paid unlock. *Note:* with the default matrix R4 is a
second line of defence (the matrix already denies those roles content); a test proves it still fires if a future edit
grants it. **Payment never overrides consent or retention** — the facade checks both even for an unlocked resource
(demo section 5: unlocked recording refused after consent withdrawal).

### 2.3 Secure storage plan (transcripts and reports)
Six stores: `transcript_store`, `report_store` (reports + score history), `recording_store`, `resume_store`,
`identity_map` (the only store holding identity + consent records), `audit_store` (append-only, no personal data).
Five principles, each a checked invariant (12 checks, all pass for the default plan):
1. **Encrypt everything personal** — AES-256-GCM envelope encryption at rest, TLS 1.2+ in transit.
2. **Keys live apart from data** — managed key service, per-tenant scope, rotation ≤ 365 days, no global key on personal data.
3. **Identity separated from content** — transcripts/reports keyed by `candidate_ref`; analytics reads scrubbed transcripts only.
4. **Deletion must actually delete** — delete the object *and* destroy its key; backups expire ≤ 35 days after.
5. **Evidence is write-once** — audit store append-only and free of personal data.
Plus coverage (every retention category has a store) and retention-policy coherence. Weakening the plan fails the
right check (demo: co-located keys → P2; raw transcripts to analytics → P3; mutable audit store → P5).
**This is a plan.** Nothing encrypts, stores, backs up or deletes a byte.

## 3. Governance documentation

### 3.1 Roles and responsibilities (proposed — to be assigned to real people)
| Role | Owns | Decides |
|---|---|---|
| Product owner | Purposes of processing, consent notices | New data uses; notice changes (bumps `required_notice_version`) |
| Engineering lead | Modules, tests, demo evidence | Releases; wiring enforcement points |
| Security / platform owner | Keys, storage, access matrix | Matrix changes; key rotation; the audit-anchor holder |
| Data protection contact (DPO-equivalent) | Retention periods, lawful basis, requests from candidates | Period changes; legal holds |
| Human reviewers (recruiters) | Every final decision | Confirm / override; **only humans confirm rejections** |
| Auditor | Periodic review of the audit log and access denials | Escalations |

### 3.2 Change control
* Threshold, weight and retention changes are **governed changes**: written reason, named approver, entry in the
  audit log (`POLICY_CHANGE` event type exists), and a re-run of the Day 54 consistency audit.
* Every score log carries `model_version` and `config_version`, so any decision can be traced to the exact logic that
  produced it. Version strings are only as good as the discipline of bumping them.
* The permission matrix and retention rules are data, not code paths: a change is a diff someone can review.

### 3.3 Candidate rights handling (logic available, process to be built)
Access to own data (candidate role), withdrawal of consent (built), erasure (retention + pseudonym unlinking),
explanation of a decision (rule trace + Day 43 explanations), human review of an AI-assisted decision (always
available). The intake/self-service screens do not exist yet.

### 3.4 Incident response outline
Detect (access-denial spikes, failed chain verification) → contain (revoke access, freeze the affected store) →
assess (which categories and candidates; the audit log and pseudonyms make this scoping possible) → notify
(regimes such as GDPR expect regulator notice within about 72 hours of awareness; confirm the rules that apply
to each deployment) → review (root cause; update controls). Unrehearsed; no on-call or tooling exists.

### 3.5 Review cadence (suggested)
Quarterly: access matrix and denials; annually or on any law change: retention periods and consent notices;
on every release: `python run_day55_governance_demo.py` and the full test suite.

## 5. Known limits and open decisions
* **Nothing is enforced yet.** No database, key service, login, intake screen or deletion job exists. Overall status
  remains **not production-compliance-ready**: logic exists, enforcement points do not.
* The audit log is in-memory with JSONL export; the chain alone does not catch truncation or a keyless full rewrite
  (the demo shows both); an external anchor and/or HMAC key must be operated.
* Retention periods, the permission matrix, and the 35-day backup window are reasoned defaults, not legal or
  security sign-off. Jurisdiction (India DPDP Act, GDPR for EU candidates, local labour law) is not modelled.
* Authentication is assumed; the `Actor` passed in is trusted.
* Pseudonymisation is not anonymisation: with the key and the mapping a person can be re-identified, by design.
* Open decisions: object store / database / region; key service and owner; who holds the audit anchor;
  per-tenant vs per-record keys.
